MASIVA-EC S.A. (hereinafter, MASIVA) is a business messaging provider. Through our platforms, companies and institutions send SMS, WhatsApp Business Platform API messages, emails and virtual assistant conversations to their own customers and users. That same technology is what criminals try to imitate to impersonate brands, banks and public institutions. This page explains how to recognize a legitimate communication, what to do about a suspicious message and what controls MASIVA applies so that its platforms are not used to deceive anyone.

1. What MASIVA does and does not do

MASIVA sends messages only on behalf of identified clients with whom it has a contractual relationship and whose identity it has verified. The content and recipient lists belong to each client, which is responsible for them.

MASIVA does not do the following:

  • It does not sell, rent or share contact databases.
  • It does not send messages on its own initiative to people who are not MASIVA clients or prospects.
  • It does not ask the recipients of its clients' messages for passwords, verification codes, card numbers or banking details.
  • It does not make calls or send messages requesting payments on behalf of third parties.
  • It does not offer jobs, prizes or loans through bulk messages.

If you receive a message asking for any of these things and mentioning MASIVA, it is a fraud attempt.

2. How to verify that a communication comes from MASIVA

Our only official channels are:

  • Websites: masiva.ec and chatbot.ec, and their subdomains.
  • Email: addresses ending in @masiva.ec. No other ending (such as @gmail.com, @hotmail.com or domains that look like masiva) belongs to MASIVA.
  • WhatsApp: verified lines that identify themselves as MASIVA. If in doubt, confirm the number by writing to info@masiva.ec before replying.

About payments: MASIVA quotes and invoices are always issued in the name of MASIVA-EC S.A., tax ID (RUC) 1793057519001, and payments are received only in bank accounts in that company's name or through the national or international payment gateways enabled on our official websites. Never accept a change of bank account communicated by email, WhatsApp or phone without confirming it through another official channel. If you receive an invoice or payment details that do not match this, do not pay and write to us at admin@masiva.ec.

About online payments: when you pay by card on our websites, the transaction is processed by an authorized national or international payment gateway, which applies its own anti-fraud controls. MASIVA does not store your full card details. Depending on the result of that validation, the payment may be approved, placed under review (in which case we may contact you through our official channels to confirm the purchase) or declined and voided if unauthorized use is detected. MASIVA will never ask you for your card's security code or passwords by phone, email or WhatsApp to complete a payment.

About job offers: MASIVA does not recruit staff through bulk messages and does not ask for money, deposits or "guarantees" to access a job opening. Every real opening is published on our official channels and the process is handled from @masiva.ec email addresses.

3. If you received a message from a company sent through our platforms

Many legitimate messages from banks, credit unions, retailers and institutions arrive through platforms like MASIVA's. In those cases, the sender is the company writing to you, not MASIVA. Some guidelines:

  • A legitimate notification informs; it does not ask you to hand over passwords, codes or card details in the same message.
  • If the message contains a link, check that it leads to the company's official domain. If in doubt, do not open it and go to the company's website by typing its address yourself.
  • If you no longer want to receive messages from a company, reply with the opt-out word indicated in the message or tell the company directly. You can also write to us at datospersonales@masiva.ec and we will handle your request in accordance with our Privacy Policy.

4. Signs of a fraudulent message

Fraud by SMS (known as smishing), WhatsApp and email (phishing) usually share these characteristics:

  • Urgency or threats: "your account will be blocked", "you have a pending fine", "your package will be returned", "last hours".
  • Requests for sensitive data: passwords, one-time verification codes, full card numbers, expiration date or security code.
  • Shortened links or domains that look similar but are not identical to the institution's (one letter changed, an extra hyphen, a different ending).
  • Generic senders or unknown numbers, including international numbers.
  • Prizes, refunds, tax returns or jobs you did not apply for.
  • Spelling mistakes, odd wording or low-quality logos.
  • Requests to pay a "small fee" to release a shipment, a prize or a procedure.

Financial institutions, the SRI (tax authority), the ANT (transit agency), the IESS (social security) and the Civil Registry do not ask for passwords or payments through links sent by message. If a message does, it is fake even if it uses the institution's name and logo.

5. What to do if you receive a suspicious message

  1. Do not open links or download attachments.
  2. Do not reply or call the number in the message.
  3. Save a screenshot showing the sender, date and time.
  4. Block and report the sender from your messaging app or WhatsApp.
  5. If the message impersonates a company you know, alert it directly through its official channels.
  6. If you shared data or made a payment, contact your bank immediately to block cards or accounts.

Where to report in Ecuador:

  • Attorney General's Office (Fiscalía General del Estado): file a report online from its website or at any prosecutor's office. Fraud by electronic means is a crime under the Organic Comprehensive Criminal Code.
  • National Police: specialized cybercrime unit.
  • EcuCERT, the computer incident response center of the Telecommunications Regulation and Control Agency (ARCOTEL), to report fraudulent message campaigns.
  • The Superintendency of Banks or the Superintendency of Popular and Solidarity Economy, if the message impersonates a financial institution.

If the message mentions MASIVA or you suspect it was sent through our platforms, write to us at datospersonales@masiva.ec with the screenshot. We will review the case and, where appropriate, apply the measures in the next section.

6. How MASIVA prevents fraudulent use of its platforms

MASIVA applies controls at every stage of the service. They are described in more detail in our Acceptable Use Policy and our Terms and Conditions.

  1. Client verification. Before enabling an account, MASIVA verifies the client's identity: company name, tax ID, legal representative and contact details. Anonymous accounts or accounts in the name of unidentified third parties are not enabled.
  2. Prior approval of senders and content. SMS sender IDs and texts go through approval by the telecommunications carriers before sending. WhatsApp Business Platform API templates are reviewed and approved by Meta. Emails are sent from authenticated domains.
  3. Prohibited content. Our Acceptable Use Policy expressly prohibits impersonation, phishing, requests for passwords or financial data, distribution of malicious software and any misleading or illegal content.
  4. Monitoring and response. MASIVA monitors campaign indicators (such as bounces, complaints and blocks) and handles the abuse reports it receives. When there are signs of fraud, it immediately suspends the account involved, with no right to a refund, and keeps the corresponding evidence.
  5. Cooperation with authorities. MASIVA cooperates with requests from the Attorney General's Office, the National Police, ARCOTEL and other competent authorities, within the legal framework and Ecuador's Organic Law on Personal Data Protection.
  6. Data protection. Recipient lists are processed only for the purpose indicated by each client and in accordance with the Privacy and Personal Data Protection Policy. MASIVA keeps a Suppression List of numbers and addresses that have asked not to receive further messages.

These controls reduce the risk, but no provider can guarantee that a third party will not try to imitate a legitimate message outside its platforms. That is why the recommendations in sections 2, 4 and 5 are the best defense.

7. Recommendations for our clients

Our clients' accounts can also be targets of fraud. We recommend:

  • Turning on two-step verification for platform access and for the associated WhatsApp Business, Meta and email accounts.
  • Not sharing usernames or passwords between people; creating one login per user and removing it when someone leaves the organization.
  • Approving campaigns, texts and top-ups only from the authorized contacts designated in the current contract or agreement.
  • Being wary of emails asking to change the bank account for payments to MASIVA or a supplier; always confirm through another channel.
  • Periodically reviewing sending reports and alerting MASIVA to any campaign they do not recognize.
  • Using their own verified senders and domains, so their customers can tell their messages apart from fake ones.
